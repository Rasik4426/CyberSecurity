**1. Cybersecurity Basics**



**Introduction:**

Cybersecurity is the process of protecting computers, networks, systems, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, theft, and damage. It helps keep personal and organizational information safe.



***CIA Triad***

The CIA Triad is the foundation of cybersecurity and consists of three important principles:



**1. Confidentiality**

Confidentiality means that information should only be accessible to authorized users.



Methods used:

Passwords

Encryption

Access control



Example: Only a student can access their own college portal account.



**2. Integrity**

Integrity ensures that data remains accurate and is not changed by unauthorized users.



Methods used:

Hashing

Digital signatures

Checksums



Example: Exam marks should not be altered without authorization.



**3. Availability**

Availability means that systems and data should be available whenever authorized users need them.



Methods used:

Data backups

Redundant servers

Disaster recovery plans



Example: A bank's website should be available 24/7 for customers.



***Common Cyber Threats***



**1. Phishing**

Fake emails or websites used to steal personal information such as passwords and bank details.



**2. Malware**

Malicious software designed to harm computers.



Types:



Virus

Worm

Trojan

Spyware



**3. DDoS Attack**

An attack that floods a server with traffic, making it unavailable to users.



**4. SQL Injection**

An attack where malicious SQL commands are inserted into a website to access or modify database information.



**5. Brute Force Attack**

Trying many password combinations until the correct password is found.



**6. Ransomware**

Malware that locks or encrypts files and demands money to restore access.







***Attack Vectors***

Attack vectors are the paths or methods attackers use to gain access to a system.



**1. Social Engineering**

Tricking people into revealing sensitive information.



Example: Pretending to be a bank employee to obtain passwords.



**2. Wireless Attacks**

Attacks targeting Wi-Fi networks and wireless devices.



Example: Cracking a weak Wi-Fi password.



**3. Insider Threats**

Threats caused by employees or trusted users who misuse their access.



Example: An employee leaking confidential company data.



**Key Points to Remember**

Cybersecurity protects systems and data from attacks.

The CIA Triad consists of Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

Common threats include Phishing, Malware, DDoS, SQL Injection, Brute Force, and Ransomware.

Attack vectors include Social Engineering, Wireless Attacks, and Insider Threats.
