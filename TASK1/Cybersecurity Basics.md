#  Cybersecurity Basics

## Objective

To understand the fundamental concepts of cybersecurity, including the CIA Triad, common cyber threats, and attack vectors used by attackers to compromise systems.

---

# Introduction

Cybersecurity is the process of protecting computers, networks, systems, and digital information from unauthorized access, attacks, theft, and damage. It plays a critical role in safeguarding personal, organizational, and government data from cybercriminals.

As technology continues to evolve, cybersecurity has become essential for maintaining privacy, confidentiality, and the integrity of digital systems.

---

# CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundation of information security and consists of three core principles:

* **Confidentiality**
* **Integrity**
* **Availability**

These principles help organizations design secure systems and protect valuable information.

---

## 1. Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that sensitive information is accessible only to authorized individuals.

### Methods Used

* Password Protection
* Encryption
* Access Control Mechanisms
* Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

### Example

Only a student can access their own college portal account using valid login credentials.

### Importance

* Protects personal and organizational data.
* Prevents unauthorized access.
* Maintains privacy and trust.

---

## 2. Integrity

Integrity ensures that data remains accurate, consistent, and unchanged unless modified by authorized users.

### Methods Used

* Hashing
* Digital Signatures
* Checksums
* Data Validation

### Example

Student examination results should not be modified without proper authorization.

### Importance

* Maintains data accuracy.
* Prevents unauthorized modifications.
* Ensures reliability of information.

---

## 3. Availability

Availability ensures that systems, applications, and data remain accessible whenever authorized users need them.

### Methods Used

* Data Backups
* Redundant Servers
* Load Balancing
* Disaster Recovery Plans

### Example

A bank's online banking portal should be available to customers 24 hours a day.

### Importance

* Reduces downtime.
* Ensures continuous service.
* Improves user satisfaction.

---

# Common Cyber Threats

Cyber threats are malicious activities aimed at damaging, stealing, or gaining unauthorized access to systems and data.

---

## 1. Phishing

Phishing is a social engineering attack where attackers use fake emails, messages, or websites to steal sensitive information.

### Target Information

* Usernames
* Passwords
* Credit Card Details
* Banking Information

### Example

A fake email pretending to be from a bank asking users to verify their account details.

---

## 2. Malware

Malware refers to malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to systems.

### Types of Malware

#### Virus

Attaches itself to legitimate files and spreads when the file is executed.

#### Worm

Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks without user interaction.

#### Trojan

Malware disguised as legitimate software.

#### Spyware

Secretly collects user information and activity.

---

## 3. DDoS Attack (Distributed Denial of Service)

A DDoS attack overwhelms a server or network with excessive traffic, making services unavailable to legitimate users.

### Impact

* Website downtime
* Reduced performance
* Service disruption

---

## 4. SQL Injection

SQL Injection occurs when attackers insert malicious SQL commands into a web application's input fields.

### Impact

* Database manipulation
* Unauthorized access
* Data theft

### Example

Accessing sensitive user records from a website database.

---

## 5. Brute Force Attack

A brute force attack involves repeatedly trying different password combinations until the correct password is discovered.

### Impact

* Account compromise
* Unauthorized access
* Data breaches

---

## 6. Ransomware

Ransomware is malware that encrypts files or systems and demands payment to restore access.

### Impact

* Data loss
* Financial loss
* Operational disruption

### Example

An organization losing access to its files until a ransom is paid.

---

# Attack Vectors

Attack vectors are the methods or pathways attackers use to gain access to systems and networks.

---

## 1. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating individuals into revealing confidential information.

### Example

An attacker pretending to be a bank employee and asking for account credentials.

### Common Techniques

* Phishing
* Impersonation
* Pretexting
* Baiting

---

## 2. Wireless Attacks

Wireless attacks target Wi-Fi networks and wireless devices.

### Example

Cracking a weak Wi-Fi password to gain unauthorized network access.

### Risks

* Data interception
* Unauthorized access
* Network compromise

---

## 3. Insider Threats

Insider threats originate from employees, contractors, or trusted users who misuse their access privileges.

### Example

An employee leaking confidential company information.

### Risks

* Data theft
* Financial damage
* Reputation loss
